\section*{Data, hypotheses, and assumptions}

The observed sequence is
\[
  D=(H,H,H,T,T,H),
  \qquad n=6,\qquad k=4\text{ heads and }2\text{ tails}.
\]
Let $\theta$ be the probability of heads on one flip. The hypotheses in the
assignment are
\[
  H_0:\theta=\tfrac12
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  H_A:\theta\in[0,1]\text{ is uncertain}.
\]
I assume that, conditional on a common $\theta$, the six flips are independent
Bernoulli trials. To define the Bayesian alternative, I choose
$\theta\mid H_A\sim\operatorname{Beta}(1,1)$, whose density is uniform on
$[0,1]$. For a decision, I assign $P(H_0)=P(H_A)=1/2$ and choose the hypothesis
with the larger posterior probability (the MAP rule). The assignment specifies
none of these prior or decision choices, so the numerical decision below is
conditional on them.

\section*{Likelihood and model evidence}

For the particular ordered sequence $D$, conditional independence gives
\[
  p(D\mid\theta)=\theta^4(1-\theta)^2.
\]
Under the fixed fair-coin hypothesis,
\[
  p(D\mid H_0)
  =\left(\tfrac12\right)^4\left(\tfrac12\right)^2
  =\frac{1}{64}.
\]
Under $H_A$, the model evidence averages the likelihood over the stated
parameter prior. Because the $\operatorname{Beta}(1,1)$ density equals $1$ on
$[0,1]$,
\begin{align*}
  p(D\mid H_A)
    &=\int_0^1 \theta^4(1-\theta)^2\,d\theta \\
    &=\int_0^1 (\theta^4-2\theta^5+\theta^6)\,d\theta \\
    &=\frac15-\frac13+\frac17
     =\frac{1}{105}.
\end{align*}
These two evidences describe the same ordered data. If the data are instead
represented only by the count of four heads, each evidence is multiplied by
$\binom64=15$, leaving their ratio unchanged.

\section*{Comparison and decision}

The Bayes factor, oriented as alternative versus null, is
\[
  \mathrm{BF}_{A0}
  =\frac{p(D\mid H_A)}{p(D\mid H_0)}
  =\frac{1/105}{1/64}
  =\frac{64}{105}\approx0.610.
\]
Thus these data favor $H_0$ over $H_A$ by a factor of $105/64\approx1.64$.
With equal prior probabilities for the hypotheses, Bayes' rule gives
\begin{align*}
  P(H_0\mid D)
    &=\frac{(1/64)(1/2)}{(1/64)(1/2)+(1/105)(1/2)}
     =\frac{105}{169}\approx0.621, \\
  P(H_A\mid D)
    &=\frac{64}{169}\approx0.379.
\end{align*}
The MAP rule therefore selects $H_0$: under the stated assumptions, the six
flips provide modest evidence in favor of the fair-coin model. This decision
does not establish that the coin is fair with certainty.

The prior probabilities matter. For general prior odds
$r=P(H_A)/P(H_0)$, the posterior odds are
\[
  \frac{P(H_A\mid D)}{P(H_0\mid D)}=\frac{64}{105}\,r.
\]
The MAP rule would instead select $H_A$ if $r>105/64$. Consequently, the
assignment's hypotheses and six observations alone do not determine a unique
Bayesian decision without a prior and decision rule. Although the stated
interval for $H_A$ includes $\theta=1/2$, a continuous uniform prior assigns
zero probability to that single value within $H_A$; $H_0$ remains a distinct
model concentrated at $\theta=1/2$.
