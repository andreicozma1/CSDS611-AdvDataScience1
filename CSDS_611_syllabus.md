Course description: Selected topics in mathematical Data Science. Topics include: foundations of Artificial Intelligence, supervised Machine Learning, unsupervised Machine Learning, reinforcement learning, statistical learning, artificial neural networks, graph neural networks, Bayesian networks, physics informed models, linear and non-linear time series analysis, data assimilation and forecasting, parametric and nonparametric methods, generation and analysis of synthetic and field data, surrogate models and simulation, development and implementation of Monte Carlo and Markov chain Monte Carlo algorithms.

Recommended Background: CSDS 511 or CSDS 612 or any course in the sequences CSDS 513/514 or CSDS 515/516 (equivalent to Math 525/526).

Course objectives: This is an advanced topics course with a special focus on the principles and methods of Bayesian Optimization (BO). The course is designed for students seeking a comprehensive, method-oriented, understanding of BO as a tool for active learning and an integral component in modern data science and AI workflows. The course will combine rigorous mathematical foundations with specialized algorithmic procedures and practical computational implementation. Students completing this course should expect to gain both conceptual mastery and practical skills for applying and improving BO in advanced data settings.

Course information: We will introduce and study the core modeling and decision-making loop in BO, including probabilistic surrogate modeling and sequential, acquisition-driven experimentation, with attention to both theoretical and computational aspects. A rigorous treatment of Gaussian processes and Bayesian decision theory will be provided, along with practical implementation details and special considerations allowing for high-performance computing.

## Course assignments

- Homework assignments: Throughout the semester, we will have 3 assignment sets with due dates roughly every 6-8 lectures. Assignments should be typed and handed in electronically in PDF format via Canvas. In preparing your reports, you can use any software you prefer.
- Assignment due: Assignment due dates depend on how much material we will cover in each lecture. For this reason, I will be posting specific due dates during the semester.
- In-class project: This course will conclude with an in-class project assignment. Each project will be completed during class time in groups of 2-3 students. Group reports should be typed and handed in electronically in PDF format like the homework assignments.
- Teamwork: In preparing your homework assignments, I encourage you to discuss the problems and seek the solutions together with your classmates. However, I expect each student to submit for grading their own solutions. In preparing the in-class project, I expect students to work in small groups and submit only a single report per group.
